Academic Statement

Why Computer Science

01

What Drew Me to Computer Science

The first time I wrote a Python script that actually solved a real problem — automating a repetitive calculation for a school project — I realized that programming was not just an abstract skill but a tool that compounds. Every problem I encountered afterward, whether in math class or in building a Telegram bot, reinforced the same idea: the right algorithm and data structure can reduce hours of manual work to seconds. That concrete realization is what drew me to pursue computer science formally, not a vague fascination with technology.

02

From Theory to Practice at Iqtidor Academy

At Iqtidor Academy, I spent one year working as a back-end developer, building REST APIs with Django and FastAPI, designing PostgreSQL schemas, and writing Telegram bot automation using Aiogram. One concrete problem I solved was optimizing a database query that was slowing down an internal reporting endpoint — by restructuring the query and adding proper indexing, I reduced response time for that endpoint. This experience taught me that backend engineering is not just about writing code, but about understanding how systems behave under real constraints.

03

Academic & Career Goals

A Computer Science undergraduate program will give me the theoretical foundation I currently lack — particularly in algorithms, data structures, distributed systems, and computational theory. While my practical experience has taught me to build functional systems, I want to understand why certain approaches work and when they fail. My goal is to work on backend infrastructure at scale, where systems design decisions directly affect millions of users. A rigorous CS education is the necessary next step to get there.
